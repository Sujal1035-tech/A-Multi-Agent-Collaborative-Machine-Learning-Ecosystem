## ✅ Key Recommendations
1. **Implement Feature Imputation and Outlier Treatment:** Prioritize imputing the 1.51% missing values in `horsepower` (e.g., using median or K-nearest neighbors imputation). Treat outliers in `horsepower` and `acceleration` (e.g., using Winsorization or robust scaling) to prevent degradation of linear model performance and improve tree splitting robustness.
2. **Deploy Ensemble Models:** Given the superior generalization performance, deploy either the **Random Forest** or **Gradient Boosting** model. Gradient Boosting shows a slightly lower variance between training and CV scores, suggesting better stability, making it a robust candidate for production.
3. **Focus on Time-Based Analysis:** Leverage `model_year` as the primary driver for forecasting future MPG improvements or benchmarking performance. Further analysis should explore interactions between `model_year` and physical attributes to understand how engineering improvements compensated for increasing vehicle size/power over time.

## ⚠️ Potential Issues & Warnings
- **Overfitting Risk in Random Forest:** Although Random Forest has the highest CV score (0.8406), the significant gap between its Training Score (0.9142) and CV Score suggests it is slightly overfitting the training data. Regularization or hyperparameter tuning (e.g., limiting tree depth) should be explored.
- **Skewed Predictors:** Several crucial features (`horsepower`, `displacement`, `weight`, `cylinders`) exhibit moderate to high positive skewness. Applying log or square root transformations to these variables could improve the performance of linear models (LR, Ridge, Lasso) if future linear modeling efforts are required.
- **High Cardinality Feature:** The `name` feature has 305 unique values, which, despite having low overall feature importance (0.307), can complicate one-hot encoding if not properly managed (e.g., grouping by manufacturer or using target encoding).
